\section{Introduction}

Pairing a language model with an external solver, tool or retrieval index
reliably raises accuracy on tasks the model cannot do alone. Systems that
translate a natural-language problem into a formal representation, dispatch it
to a symbolic engine, and return the engine's verdict through the model report
large end-to-end gains on logical reasoning
\citep{pan2023logiclm,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm}, on arithmetic and
algorithmic tasks solved by an interpreter \citep{gao2022pal}, on API-mediated
subtasks \citep{schick2023toolformer}, on formal theorem proving
\citep{yang2023leandojo}, and on knowledge-intensive generation supported by
retrieved passages \citep{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}. Those gains are
routinely read as evidence that the model \emph{used} the supplied material.

That reading is underdetermined. At least two mechanisms produce the same
accuracy number. Under \emph{answer reading}, the supplied record contains the
conclusion, or something that co-occurs with it, and the model copies out the
implied response. Under \emph{state use}, the model works with the supplied
premises and completes the remaining inference itself. The first says nothing
about what the model does with reasoning; the second says something specific.
The standard control in this literature is a same-shape but irrelevant record,
which rules out proof length, generic validity and formatting. It does not rule
out answer reading, because a \emph{relevant} certificate stays
answer-diagnostic even when the response token is withheld: a prefix that stops
one step short still names the query's subject and predicate in adjacent lines,
so a model that only checks surface co-occurrence reproduces every number a
reasoning model would.

Separating the two requires a control that holds surface form fixed and
destroys validity alone. This paper builds one. Three sealed experiments on
frozen small models score responses by direct next-token likelihood over
verified single-token candidates, so no free text is parsed and no verdict is
inferred from prose. The first reads a completed five-arm certificate factorial
as a $2\times2$ of \emph{answer evidence} $\times$ \emph{proof state}, which
separates the two channels that end-to-end accuracy conflates. The second holds
line count, line types, query-entity occurrence count, query-predicate presence
and token length fixed across ten arms and breaks only the logic, which splits
the state effect into a surface-overlap component and a validity-tracking
component, and additionally varies entity repetition, line order and truncation
depth, and supplies a valid-looking certificate whose fabricated final rule
points the wrong way. The third scores ten models on a three-class panel whose
\emph{undetermined} items are certified by forward-closure saturation, and asks
which models can serve as the interface at all.

The picture that emerges is narrower than the headline accuracy suggests.
Holding surface form fixed and breaking only validity removes almost the entire
state effect: the validity component is $+60.4$pp against a surface component
of $+1.0$pp, so $98.3\%$ of the state effect is inference over the supplied
lines rather than text matching. Entity repetition contributes nothing --- a
valid derivation about the query entity toward an unrelated predicate scores
identically ($+0.0$pp) to one about a different entity, despite naming the query
subject four times rather than zero. The inference is exactly one step deep:
withholding two steps instead of one scores identically to supplying no
certificate at all, a cliff rather than a slope. And the tracking confers no
robustness: handed a valid-looking certificate whose fabricated final rule
establishes the negation, the model follows it on 189 of 192 items. Across ten
models, three clear a per-class recall floor; below 1.7B parameters models
collapse to a single label whatever the solver supplies, and the largest model
tested collapses while a 3B model does not.

Two measurement points shape how any of this may be reported. First, accuracy
alone is not interpretable here: one arm scored $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel
with $d' = 0.00$, matching a trivial always-one-label strategy while
discriminating nothing, so sensitivity and criterion are reported alongside
accuracy throughout \citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}.
Paired contrasts use the exact McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947note} and
bias-corrected accelerated bootstrap intervals \citep{efron1987better}. Second,
the claim boundary is strict and is maintained everywhere in this paper. Every
arm without a near-complete supplied chain returns $d' = 0$, so the models
studied do not reason unaided; high accuracy with full solver material describes
a faithful transcriber, not a reasoner. System-level effects are never upgraded
into claims about the model's own reasoning.

The contributions are: (i) an \emph{answer-evidence decomposition} that
separates solver-supplied answers from solver-supplied reasoning state, reusable
for any tool-augmented system; (ii) a \emph{surface-matched invalidity control}
that isolates logical validity from every co-occurring surface cue, with
per-item certification that the displayed lines do not settle the query; (iii) a
\emph{forward-closure certifier} for constructing formally underdetermined
items, validated at $23{,}240/23{,}240$ against an independently labelled
corpus \citep{tafjord2020proofwriter}; (iv) evidence that validity tracking is
real, shallow, and offers no protection against corrupted solver output, a
reliability result for the whole class of solver-in-the-loop architectures; and
(v) a substrate-size floor from a ten-model sweep spanning open instruction-tuned
families \citep{team2024qwen,grattafiori2024llama,team2025gemma,microsoft2025phi,team2024olmo,allal2025smollm}.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Faithfulness of displayed reasoning}

Chain-of-thought prompting made intermediate reasoning steps a standard part of
the model's output \citep{wei2022chainofthought}, and a body of work then asked
whether those steps are what the answer actually rests on.
\citet{turpin2023language} show that explanations systematically fail to mention
biasing features that demonstrably drive the prediction, so a plausible trace
can misrepresent the true cause. \citet{lanham2023measuring} intervene directly
on the trace --- truncating it so the model must answer early, and injecting
mistakes into it --- and read the resulting change in the answer as a measure of
how strongly the answer is conditioned on the displayed reasoning; they find
wide variation across tasks and models, and that mistakes are often ignored.
The same manipulations have since been applied to naturally worded prompts
without artificial bias \citep{arcuschin2025chainofthought} and to models trained
to produce long reasoning traces, where hints that determine the answer are
verbalised only a minority of the time \citep{chen2025reasoning}. A separate
response to the problem is architectural: \citet{lyu2023faithful} make the trace
a formal program executed by a deterministic solver, so the displayed chain is
faithful to the answer by construction.

The limitation this paper addresses is one of object rather than of method.
This literature intervenes on \emph{model-generated} traces, where the question
is whether the model's own account is honest. It does not address
\emph{externally supplied, formally verified} state, where the material is
correct by construction and the question inverts: does the model track the
validity of a derivation it did not produce? This paper adapts the truncation
and mistake-injection manipulations to solver certificates, and adds a control
this literature does not use --- an invalid certificate matched to a valid one on
every surface property, with per-item certification that the displayed lines
leave the query underdetermined. Adapting the manipulation also changes what a
null means: a model that ignores an injected mistake in its own trace is
unfaithful, whereas a model that follows a corrupted external certificate is
unsafe.

\subsection{Solver-augmented and tool-augmented reasoning}

Neurosymbolic pipelines put the formal step outside the model. Logic-LM
translates a problem into a task-specific symbolic language, calls a solver, and
refines the formalisation from solver error messages \citep{pan2023logiclm};
LINC has the model act as a semantic parser into first-order logic and offloads
deduction to a theorem prover \citep{olausson2023linc}; SatLM generates a
declarative specification and dispatches it to an automated theorem prover
\citep{ye2023satlm}; PAL emits a program and defers execution to an interpreter
\citep{gao2022pal}. Related work extends the pattern to self-supervised API use
\citep{schick2023toolformer}, to retrieval-augmented premise selection in
interactive theorem proving \citep{yang2023leandojo}, and to retrieval as a
general source of external evidence \citep{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}.

These systems report end-to-end accuracy and attribute the gain to the pipeline.
Two things are consequently left open. First, the contribution of the
\emph{reasoning state} is not separated from the contribution of the
\emph{answer}: because the solver's output normally contains both, no reported
number distinguishes a model that reasons over supplied premises from one that
reads off a supplied conclusion. Second, the solver is assumed correct, so the
behaviour of the pipeline under a wrong-but-well-formed solver output is not
measured. Adjacent work shows the model side is not neutral to what the context
contains: irrelevant material inserted into a problem degrades accuracy
\citep{shi2023large}, and models are highly receptive to coherent external
evidence that contradicts their parametric knowledge \citep{xie2023adaptive}.
This paper decomposes the solver's contribution into an answer channel and a
state channel, measures each on matched arms, and adds an arm in which the
certificate is valid-looking but points the wrong way. The state channel carries
real validity information; it supplies no protection against a corrupted
apparatus.

\subsection{Synthetic deductive reasoning benchmarks}

The task family used here descends from rule-based deductive datasets stated in
natural language. RuleTaker showed that transformers can assign truth values to
candidate implications of a theory expressed in language and generalise to
deeper chaining than seen in training \citep{clark2020transformers}. ProofWriter
added generated proofs and open-world semantics with an explicit unknown class
\citep{tafjord2020proofwriter}. PrOntoQA generates items from a synthetic world
model in first-order logic so that a generated chain can be parsed into a
symbolic proof and analysed step by step, and finds that individual deduction
steps are usually correct while proof planning is not
\citep{saparov2022language}. FOLIO supplies human-written premises with verified
first-order logic annotations \citep{han2022folio}. Two properties of these
resources matter for the present design: order effects are known to be large in
deductive prompting \citep{chen2024premise}, and the sets are public and predate
the models evaluated on them, so contamination cannot be excluded --- a problem
documented for modern benchmarks generally
\citep{deng2024investigating,golchin2023time} and sharpened by the finding that
performance can drop sharply on counterfactual variants of familiar tasks
\citep{wu2023reasoning}.

The gap is that these benchmarks supply items and proofs but not the matched
adversarial certificate variants an attribution question needs. This paper
generates items after model release with per-item nonce vocabularies, and
constructs the matched invalid, misleading, order-scrambled and depth-truncated
certificates that the public sets do not provide. A public open-world corpus is
still used, but only as an independent check on the forward-closure certifier
that labels the underdetermined items; no item from it is scored by any model
here.

\subsection{Response bias and the limits of accuracy}

Prompted classification is well documented to be biased rather than merely
noisy. \citet{zhao2021calibrate} show that few-shot accuracy swings from near
chance to near state of the art with prompt format and example order because the
model carries a prior toward particular answer strings, and propose estimating
and removing that prior. Option order alone moves multiple-choice accuracy
substantially \citep{pezeshkpour2023large}, and the effect traces to a token-level
prior over option identifiers rather than to the content of the options
\citep{zheng2023large}. Scoring by raw likelihood introduces a further
confound, since strings compete for probability mass independently of their
correctness \citep{holtzman2021surface}. On the abstention side, models are
partially able to assess whether their own answers are right
\citep{kadavath2022language}, and can be evaluated on whether they recognise
questions that admit no answer \citep{yin2023large}.

What this literature diagnoses, evaluation practice in solver-augmented
reasoning has not absorbed: bare accuracy remains the reported quantity. On a
balanced panel, a degenerate single-label responder is indistinguishable from a
chance-level competent one, and both are indistinguishable from a genuine
partial discriminator with a shifted criterion. Signal detection theory
separates exactly these
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}, and this paper
therefore reports $d'$ and criterion $c$ alongside accuracy for every arm, with
paired significance from the exact McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947note} and
interval estimates from the bias-corrected accelerated bootstrap
\citep{efron1987better}. Two concrete cases motivate the practice rather than
illustrate it: a baseline arm scoring $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel turns out to
have $d' = 0.00$, and a prespecified qualification criterion based on a single
class recall was passed by models that answer one label almost everywhere. Both
were caught only because sensitivity was reported separately from accuracy; one
of the two is a criterion of this paper's own, replaced post hoc and labelled as
such.
