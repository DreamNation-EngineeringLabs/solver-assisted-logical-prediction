\section{Introduction}

Pairing a language model with an external solver, tool or retrieval index
reliably raises accuracy on tasks the model cannot do alone. Systems that
translate a problem into a formal representation, dispatch it to a symbolic
engine, and return the engine's verdict through the model report large
end-to-end gains on logical reasoning
\citep{pan2023logiclm,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm}, on tasks solved by an
interpreter \citep{gao2022pal}, on API-mediated subtasks
\citep{schick2023toolformer}, on theorem proving \citep{yang2023leandojo}, and on
retrieval-supported generation \citep{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}. Those gains
are routinely read as evidence that the model \emph{used} the supplied material.

That reading is underdetermined. Two mechanisms produce the same accuracy
number. Under \emph{answer reading}, the supplied record contains the
conclusion, or something that co-occurs with it, and the model copies out the
implied response. Under \emph{state use}, the model works with the supplied
premises and completes the remaining inference itself. The first says nothing
about what the model does with reasoning; the second says something specific.
The standard control is a same-shape but irrelevant record, which rules out
proof length, generic validity and formatting. It does not rule out answer
reading, because a \emph{relevant} certificate stays answer-diagnostic even when
the response token is withheld: a prefix stopping one step short still names the
query's subject and predicate in adjacent lines, so a model that only checks
surface co-occurrence reproduces every number a reasoning model would.

Separating the two requires a control that holds surface form fixed and destroys
validity alone. This paper builds one and applies it across four sealed
experiments on frozen small models, scoring by direct next-token likelihood over
verified single-token candidates so that no free text is parsed. Experiment 1
reads a five-arm certificate factorial as a $2\times2$ of \emph{answer evidence}
$\times$ \emph{proof state}. Experiment 2 holds line count, line types,
query-entity occurrences, query-predicate presence and token length fixed across
ten arms and breaks only the logic, and additionally varies entity repetition,
line order and truncation depth and supplies a valid-looking certificate whose
fabricated final rule points the wrong way. Experiment 3 scores ten models on a
three-class panel whose \emph{undetermined} items are certified by
forward-closure saturation. Experiment 4 rescores the broken-chain items with a
third candidate, separating detection of an invalid certificate from failure to
complete one.

\textbf{The method is the contribution; what it returns is model-specific.}
Breaking validity alone removes almost the whole state effect on one checkpoint
--- a validity share of $98.3\%$ --- and $40.4\%$ on another, which answers $58$
of $96$ broken certificates correctly where the first manages $1$. We offer the
decomposition and decline to generalise the value it returns, ours included.
Three results do hold everywhere: the inference is one step deep and requires the
final rule to \emph{follow} the premise it fires on; it is \emph{not} detection,
since a broken certificate \emph{lowers} abstention; and none of the three
checkpoints resists a corrupted apparatus, answering incorrectly on $189$, $186$
and $192$ of $192$ items given a certificate whose fabricated final rule
establishes the negation.

Two measurement points shape how this may be reported. Accuracy alone is not
interpretable: one arm scored $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel at $d' = 0.00$,
matching a trivial always-one-label strategy while discriminating nothing, so
sensitivity and criterion accompany it throughout
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}, with the exact McNemar
test \citep{mcnemar1947note} and BCa intervals \citep{efron1987better}. And the
claim boundary is strict: on the Qwen checkpoints every arm without a
near-complete chain returns $d' = 0$, so those models do not reason unaided and
high accuracy with full solver material describes a faithful transcriber.
Gemma-3-4b is not in that position ($22/96$ unaided), which is why its share is
reported against two baselines. System-level effects are never upgraded into
claims about the model's own reasoning.

The contributions are: (i) an \emph{answer-evidence decomposition} separating
solver-supplied answers from solver-supplied reasoning state, reusable for any
tool-augmented system; (ii) a \emph{surface-matched invalidity control} isolating
validity from every co-occurring surface cue, certified per item; (iii) a
\emph{forward-closure certifier} for underdetermined items, validated at
$23{,}240/23{,}240$ against an independently labelled corpus
\citep{tafjord2020proofwriter} and against nine adversarial classes; (iv)
evidence that apparent use of proof state can be surface exploitation, that the
behaviour is completion rather than detection, and that no checkpoint resists a
corrupted certificate; and (v) a ten-model sweep showing viability to be a
property of (model $\times$ arm), with proof state \emph{degrading} three of ten
interfaces
\citep{team2024qwen,grattafiori2024llama,team2025gemma,microsoft2025phi,team2024olmo,allal2025smollm}.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Faithfulness of displayed reasoning}

Chain-of-thought prompting made intermediate steps part of the output
\citep{wei2022chainofthought}, and a body of work then asked whether the answer
rests on them. \citet{turpin2023language} show explanations systematically omit
biasing features that demonstrably drive the prediction.
\citet{lanham2023measuring} intervene on the trace --- truncating it, and
injecting mistakes --- and read the change in the answer as a measure of
conditioning; they find wide variation and that mistakes are often ignored. The
same manipulations have been applied to naturally worded prompts
\citep{arcuschin2025chainofthought} and to models trained for long traces, where
answer-determining hints are verbalised only a minority of the time
\citep{chen2025reasoning}; \citet{lyu2023faithful} respond architecturally,
making the trace a program executed by a solver.

The limitation is one of object rather than method. This literature intervenes on
\emph{model-generated} traces, asking whether the model's own account is honest.
It does not address \emph{externally supplied, formally verified} state, where
the question inverts: does the model track a derivation it did not produce? We
adapt truncation and mistake injection to solver certificates and add a control
this literature does not use --- an invalid certificate matched to a valid one on
every surface property, certified per item not to settle the query. That also
changes what a null means: ignoring an injected mistake in one's own trace is
unfaithful, whereas following a corrupted external certificate is unsafe.

\subsection{Solver-augmented and tool-augmented reasoning}

Neurosymbolic pipelines put the formal step outside the model. Logic-LM
translates a problem into a symbolic language and refines the formalisation from
solver errors \citep{pan2023logiclm}; LINC parses into first-order logic and
offloads deduction to a prover \citep{olausson2023linc}; SatLM dispatches a
declarative specification to a theorem prover \citep{ye2023satlm}; PAL emits a
program for an interpreter \citep{gao2022pal}. The pattern extends to
self-supervised API use \citep{schick2023toolformer}, premise selection in
interactive theorem proving \citep{yang2023leandojo}, and retrieval as general
external evidence \citep{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}.

These systems report end-to-end accuracy and attribute the gain to the pipeline,
which leaves two things open. The contribution of the \emph{reasoning state} is
not separated from that of the \emph{answer}: the solver's output normally
contains both, so no reported number distinguishes reasoning over supplied
premises from reading off a supplied conclusion. And the solver is assumed
correct, so behaviour under wrong-but-well-formed output is unmeasured. The
model side is not neutral to context: irrelevant material degrades accuracy
\citep{shi2023large}, and models are highly receptive to coherent evidence
contradicting their parametric knowledge \citep{xie2023adaptive}. We decompose
the solver's contribution into an answer channel and a state channel, measure
each on matched arms, and add an arm whose certificate is valid-looking but
points the wrong way.

\subsection{Synthetic deductive reasoning benchmarks}

The task family descends from rule-based deductive datasets in natural language.
RuleTaker showed transformers can assign truth values to implications of a
theory and generalise beyond training depth \citep{clark2020transformers}.
ProofWriter added generated proofs and open-world semantics with an explicit
unknown class \citep{tafjord2020proofwriter}. PrOntoQA generates items from a
synthetic world model so chains can be parsed into symbolic proofs, finding
individual steps usually correct while proof planning is not
\citep{saparov2022language}. FOLIO supplies human-written premises with verified
first-order annotations \citep{han2022folio}. Two properties matter here: order
effects are large in deductive prompting \citep{chen2024premise}, and the sets
are public and predate the models evaluated on them, so contamination cannot be
excluded --- documented for modern benchmarks generally
\citep{deng2024investigating,golchin2023time} and sharpened by sharp drops on
counterfactual variants of familiar tasks \citep{wu2023reasoning}.

These benchmarks supply items and proofs but not the matched adversarial
certificate variants an attribution question needs. We generate items after model
release with per-item nonce vocabularies, and construct the matched invalid,
misleading, order-scrambled and depth-truncated certificates the public sets do
not provide. A public open-world corpus is used only as an independent check on
the forward-closure certifier; no item from it is scored by any model here.

\subsection{Response bias and the limits of accuracy}

Prompted classification is biased rather than merely noisy.
Few-shot accuracy swings from near chance to near state of the art with format
and example order, because the model carries a prior toward particular answer
strings \citep{zhao2021calibrate}; option order alone moves multiple-choice
accuracy substantially \citep{pezeshkpour2023large}, tracing to a token-level
prior over option identifiers rather than option content \citep{zheng2023large};
and likelihood scoring adds a confound, since strings compete for probability
mass independently of correctness \citep{holtzman2021surface}. On abstention,
models can partially assess whether their own answers are right
\citep{kadavath2022language} and be evaluated on recognising questions that admit
no answer \citep{yin2023large}.

Evaluation practice in solver-augmented reasoning has not absorbed this: bare
accuracy remains the reported quantity. On a balanced panel a degenerate
single-label responder is indistinguishable from a chance-level competent one,
and both from a partial discriminator with a shifted criterion. Signal detection
theory separates exactly these
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}, and this paper
therefore reports $d'$ and criterion $c$ alongside accuracy for every arm, with
paired significance from the exact McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947note} and
interval estimates from the bias-corrected accelerated bootstrap
\citep{efron1987better}. Two concrete cases motivate the practice rather than
illustrate it: a baseline arm scoring $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel turns out to
have $d' = 0.00$, and a prespecified qualification criterion based on a single
class recall was passed by models that answer one label almost everywhere. Both
were caught only because sensitivity was reported separately from accuracy; one
of the two is a criterion of this paper's own, replaced post hoc and labelled as
such.
