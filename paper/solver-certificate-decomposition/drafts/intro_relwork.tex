\section{Introduction}

Pairing a language model with an external solver, tool or retrieval index
reliably raises accuracy on tasks the model cannot do alone. Systems that
translate a problem into a formal representation, dispatch it to a symbolic
engine and return the verdict through the model report large end-to-end gains
across logical reasoning, program execution, API use, theorem proving and
retrieval-supported generation
\citep{pan2023logiclm,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm,gao2022pal,schick2023toolformer,yang2023leandojo,lewis2020retrievalaugmented}.
Those gains are routinely read as evidence that the model \emph{used} the
supplied material.

That reading is underdetermined. Two mechanisms produce the same accuracy
number. Under \emph{answer reading}, the supplied record contains the
conclusion, or something that co-occurs with it, and the model copies out the
implied response. Under \emph{state use}, the model works with the supplied
premises and completes the remaining inference itself. The first says nothing
about what the model does with reasoning; the second says something specific.
The standard control is a same-shape but irrelevant record, which rules out
proof length, generic validity and formatting. It does not rule out answer
reading, because a \emph{relevant} certificate stays answer-diagnostic even when
the response token is withheld: a prefix stopping one step short still names the
query's subject and predicate in adjacent lines, so a model that only checks
surface co-occurrence reproduces every number a reasoning model would.

Separating the two requires a control that holds surface form fixed and destroys
validity alone. This paper builds one and applies it across four sealed
experiments on frozen small models, scoring by direct next-token likelihood over
verified single-token candidates so that no free text is parsed: a five-arm
factorial read as a $2\times2$ of \emph{answer evidence} $\times$ \emph{proof
state}; ten arms that hold every surface property fixed while breaking only the
logic; a three-class panel, certified by forward-closure saturation, across ten
models; and a rescoring of the broken-chain items with a third candidate. Two
robustness studies follow, on line order and on the inference runtime.

\textbf{The method is the contribution; what it returns is model-specific.}
Breaking validity alone removes almost the whole state effect on one checkpoint, a validity share of $98.3\%$, and $40.4\%$ on another, which answers $58$
of $96$ broken certificates correctly where the first manages $1$. We offer the
decomposition and decline to generalise the value it returns, ours included.
Three results do hold everywhere: the inference is one step deep and is degraded
when the final rule precedes the premise it fires on; it is \emph{not} detection,
since a broken certificate \emph{lowers} abstention; and none of the three
checkpoints resists a corrupted apparatus, answering incorrectly on $189$, $186$
and $192$ of $192$ items given a certificate whose fabricated final rule
establishes the negation.

Two measurement points shape how this may be reported. Accuracy alone is not interpretable: one arm scored $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel at $d' = 0.00$, so sensitivity and criterion accompany it throughout
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}. And the claim boundary
is strict: on the Qwen checkpoints every arm without a near-complete chain
returns $d' = 0$, so those models do not reason unaided and high accuracy with
full solver material describes a faithful transcriber. Gemma-3-4b is not in that
position ($22/96$ unaided), which is why its share is reported against two
baselines. System-level effects are never upgraded into claims about the model's
own reasoning.

The contributions are: (i) an \emph{answer-evidence decomposition} separating
solver-supplied answers from solver-supplied reasoning state; (ii) a
\emph{surface-matched invalidity control} isolating validity from every
co-occurring surface cue, certified per item; (iii) a \emph{forward-closure
certifier} for underdetermined items, validated at $23{,}240/23{,}240$ against an
independently labelled corpus \citep{tafjord2020proofwriter} and nine adversarial
classes; (iv) evidence that apparent use of proof state can be surface
exploitation, that the behaviour is completion rather than detection, and that no
checkpoint resists a corrupted certificate; and (v) a sweep over twelve models
\citep{team2024qwen,grattafiori2024llama,team2025gemma,microsoft2025phi,team2024olmo,allal2025smollm}
showing viability to be a property of (model $\times$ arm $\times$ runtime),
including one model whose verdict changes with the inference stack alone.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Faithfulness of displayed reasoning}

Chain-of-thought prompting made intermediate steps part of the output
\citep{wei2022chainofthought}, and a body of work then asked whether the answer
rests on them. \citet{turpin2023language} show explanations systematically omit
biasing features that demonstrably drive the prediction.
\citet{lanham2023measuring} intervene on the trace, truncating it and injecting mistakes, and read the change in the answer as a measure of
conditioning; they find wide variation and that mistakes are often ignored. The
same manipulations have been applied to naturally worded prompts
\citep{arcuschin2025chainofthought} and to models trained for long traces, where
answer-determining hints are verbalised only a minority of the time
\citep{chen2025reasoning}; \citet{lyu2023faithful} respond architecturally,
making the trace a program executed by a solver.

The limitation is one of object rather than method. This literature intervenes on
\emph{model-generated} traces, asking whether the model's own account is honest.
It does not address \emph{externally supplied, formally verified} state, where
the question inverts: does the model track a derivation it did not produce? We
adapt truncation and mistake injection to solver certificates and add a control
this literature does not use --- an invalid certificate matched to a valid one on
every surface property, certified per item not to settle the query. That also
changes what a null means: ignoring an injected mistake in one's own trace is
unfaithful, whereas following a corrupted external certificate is unsafe.

\subsection{Solver-augmented and tool-augmented reasoning}

Neurosymbolic pipelines put the formal step outside the model: translating into
a symbolic language and repairing from solver errors \citep{pan2023logiclm},
parsing into first-order logic for a prover \citep{olausson2023linc},
dispatching a declarative specification \citep{ye2023satlm}, or emitting a
program for an interpreter \citep{gao2022pal}. The pattern extends to
self-supervised API use \citep{schick2023toolformer}, premise selection in
interactive theorem proving \citep{yang2023leandojo}, and retrieval as general
external evidence \citep{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}.

All report end-to-end accuracy and attribute the gain to the pipeline, leaving
two things open. The \emph{reasoning state}'s contribution is not separated from
the \emph{answer}'s, since the solver's output contains both. And the solver is
assumed correct, so behaviour under wrong-but-well-formed output is unmeasured
--- though the model side is demonstrably not neutral to context, with irrelevant
material degrading accuracy \citep{shi2023large} and coherent evidence
overriding parametric knowledge \citep{xie2023adaptive}. We decompose the
solver's contribution into an answer channel and a state channel, and add an arm
whose certificate is valid-looking but points the wrong way.

\subsection{Synthetic deductive reasoning benchmarks}

The task family descends from rule-based deductive datasets in natural language:
RuleTaker \citep{clark2020transformers}, ProofWriter, which added generated
proofs and open-world semantics with an explicit unknown class
\citep{tafjord2020proofwriter}, PrOntoQA, whose chains parse into symbolic proofs
and whose individual steps are usually correct while proof planning is not
\citep{saparov2022language}, and FOLIO's human-written premises with verified
annotations \citep{han2022folio}. Two properties matter here: order effects are
large in deductive prompting \citep{chen2024premise}, and these sets are public
and predate the models evaluated on them, so contamination cannot be excluded
\citep{deng2024investigating,golchin2023time,wu2023reasoning}.

They supply items and proofs but not the matched adversarial certificate variants
an attribution question needs, so we generate items after model release with
per-item nonce vocabularies and build the matched invalid, misleading,
order-scrambled and depth-truncated certificates ourselves. A public open-world
corpus checks the forward-closure certifier only; no item from it is scored.

\subsection{Response bias and the limits of accuracy}

Prompted classification is biased rather than merely noisy.
Few-shot accuracy swings from near chance to near state of the art with format
and example order, because the model carries a prior toward particular answer
strings \citep{zhao2021calibrate}; option order alone moves multiple-choice
accuracy \citep{pezeshkpour2023large,zheng2023large}; and likelihood scoring adds
a confound, since strings compete for probability mass independently of
correctness \citep{holtzman2021surface}. On abstention, models can partially
assess their own answers \citep{kadavath2022language} and be evaluated on
recognising unanswerable questions \citep{yin2023large}.

Evaluation practice in solver-augmented reasoning has not absorbed this: bare
accuracy remains the reported quantity. On a balanced panel a degenerate
single-label responder is indistinguishable from a chance-level competent one,
and both from a partial discriminator with a shifted criterion. Signal detection
theory separates exactly these
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}, and this paper
therefore reports $d'$ and criterion $c$ alongside accuracy for every arm, with
paired significance from the exact McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947note} and
interval estimates from the bias-corrected accelerated bootstrap
\citep{efron1987better}. Two concrete cases here motivate the practice rather than
illustrate it: an arm scoring $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel has $d' = 0.00$, and a
prespecified criterion on a single class's recall was passed by models answering
one label almost everywhere. One is a criterion of our own, replaced post hoc and
labelled as such.
