\section{Introduction}

Pairing a language model with an external solver, tool or retrieval index
reliably raises accuracy on tasks the model cannot do alone. Systems that translate a problem into a formal representation, dispatch it to a
symbolic engine and return the verdict through the model report large end-to-end
gains \citep{pan2023logiclm,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm,gao2022pal,schick2023toolformer,yang2023leandojo,lewis2020retrievalaugmented}.
Those gains are routinely read as evidence that the model \emph{used} the
supplied material.

That reading is underdetermined. Two mechanisms produce the same accuracy
number. Under \emph{answer reading}, the supplied record contains the
conclusion, or something that co-occurs with it, and the model copies out the
implied response. Under \emph{state use}, the model works with the supplied
premises and completes the remaining inference itself. The first says nothing
about what the model does with reasoning; the second says something specific.
The standard control is a same-shape but irrelevant record, which rules out
proof length, generic validity and formatting. It does not rule out answer
reading, because a \emph{relevant} certificate stays answer-diagnostic even when
the response token is withheld: a prefix stopping one step short still names the
query's subject and predicate in adjacent lines, so a model that only checks
surface co-occurrence reproduces every number a reasoning model would.

Separating the two requires a control that holds surface form fixed and destroys
validity alone. This paper builds one and applies it across four sealed experiments on frozen
small models, scoring by direct next-token likelihood over verified single-token
candidates so that no free text is parsed, with two robustness studies
on line order and on the inference runtime.

\textbf{The method is the contribution; what it returns is model-specific.}
Breaking validity alone removes almost the whole state effect on one checkpoint
(a validity share of $98.3\%$) and $40.4\%$ on another, which answers $58$ of $96$
broken certificates correctly where the first manages $1$. We offer the
decomposition and decline to generalise the value it returns, ours included.
Two results do hold everywhere: accuracy is degraded when the final rule
precedes the premise it fires on, and none of the three checkpoints resists a
corrupted apparatus, answering incorrectly on $189$, $186$ and $192$ of $192$
items given a certificate whose fabricated final rule establishes the negation.
Depth is checkpoint-specific, and whether the model detects invalidity our
abstention test cannot settle in either direction.

Two measurement points shape how this may be reported. Accuracy alone is not interpretable: one arm scored $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel at $d' = 0.00$, so sensitivity and criterion accompany it throughout
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}. And the claim boundary
is strict: on the Qwen checkpoints every arm without a near-complete chain
returns $d' = 0$, so those models do not reason unaided and high accuracy with
full solver material describes a faithful transcriber. Gemma-3-4b is not in that
position ($22/96$ unaided), which is why its share is reported against two
baselines. System-level effects are never upgraded into claims about the model's
own reasoning.

The contributions are: (i) an \emph{answer-evidence decomposition} separating
solver-supplied answers from solver-supplied reasoning state; (ii) a
\emph{surface-matched invalidity control} isolating validity from every
co-occurring surface cue, certified per item; (iii) a \emph{forward-closure
certifier} for underdetermined items, validated in three layers: $23{,}240/23{,}240$ against a public corpus whose
labels share our inference fragment \citep{tafjord2020proofwriter}, nine
adversarial theories, and a differential test against an independent engine; (iv) evidence that apparent use of proof state can be surface
exploitation, that no checkpoint resists a corrupted certificate, and that the
standard abstention test cannot decide whether invalidity is detected; and (v) a sweep over twelve models
\citep{team2024qwen,grattafiori2024llama,team2025gemma,microsoft2025phi,team2024olmo,allal2025smollm}
showing viability to be a property of (model $\times$ arm $\times$ runtime), one
model's verdict changing with the inference stack alone.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Faithfulness, and the solver-augmented pipeline}

Chain-of-thought made intermediate steps part of the output
\citep{wei2022chainofthought}, and a literature then asked whether the answer
rests on them: explanations omit the biasing features that demonstrably drive the
prediction \citep{turpin2023language}; truncating a trace and injecting mistakes
into it reveals wide variation in conditioning, with injected mistakes often
ignored \citep{lanham2023measuring,arcuschin2025chainofthought}; models trained
for long traces verbalise answer-determining hints only a minority of the time
\citep{chen2025reasoning}; and one response is architectural, making the trace a
program a solver executes \citep{lyu2023faithful}. Neurosymbolic pipelines put
the formal step outside the model altogether: translating into a symbolic language and repairing from solver errors \citep{pan2023logiclm}, parsing into
first-order logic for a prover \citep{olausson2023linc}, dispatching a
declarative specification \citep{ye2023satlm}, emitting a program for an interpreter
\citep{gao2022pal}. The pattern extends to self-supervised API
use \citep{schick2023toolformer}, premise selection \citep{yang2023leandojo} and
retrieval \citep{lewis2020retrievalaugmented}.

Both leave our question open. The first intervenes on \emph{model-generated}
traces, asking whether the model's own account is honest; ours is
\emph{externally supplied, formally verified} state, where the question inverts, and where ignoring a mistake in one's own trace is unfaithful, whereas
following a corrupted external certificate is unsafe. The second reports
end-to-end accuracy, which cannot separate the solver's \emph{answer} from its
\emph{state}, and assumes the solver correct, though the model side is not
neutral to context \citep{shi2023large,xie2023adaptive}. We decompose the two
channels and add a surface-matched invalid certificate and a valid-looking one
that points the wrong way.

\subsection{Benchmarks, and the limits of accuracy}

The task family descends from rule-based deductive datasets in natural language:
RuleTaker \citep{clark2020transformers}, ProofWriter, which added open-world
semantics with an explicit unknown class \citep{tafjord2020proofwriter}, PrOntoQA
\citep{saparov2022language} and FOLIO \citep{han2022folio}. Order effects are
large in deductive prompting \citep{chen2024premise}; these sets predate the
models evaluated on them, so contamination cannot be excluded
\citep{deng2024investigating,golchin2023time}; and counterfactual variants show
how much apparent competence rests on familiarity \citep{wu2023reasoning}. None
supplies the matched adversarial certificate variants an attribution question
needs, so we generate items after model release with per-item nonce vocabularies
and build those certificates ourselves.

Prompted classification is biased rather than merely noisy: few-shot accuracy
swings from near chance to near state of the art with format and example order,
because the model carries a prior toward particular answer strings
\citep{zhao2021calibrate}; option order alone moves multiple-choice accuracy
\citep{pezeshkpour2023large,zheng2023large}; likelihood scoring adds a confound,
since strings compete for probability mass independently of correctness
\citep{holtzman2021surface}; and abstention is its own capability
\citep{kadavath2022language,yin2023large}. Solver-augmented evaluation has not absorbed this: on a balanced panel a
degenerate single-label responder is indistinguishable from a chance-level
competent one. Signal detection theory separates them
\citep{macmillan1991detection,stanislaw1999calculation}, so we report $d'$ and
criterion $c$ for every arm, with the exact McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947note}
and bootstrap intervals \citep{efron1987better}. Two concrete cases here motivate the practice
rather than illustrate it: an arm scoring $50.0\%$ on a balanced panel has
$d' = 0.00$, and a prespecified criterion on a single class's recall was passed by
models answering one label almost everywhere. One is a criterion of our own,
replaced post hoc and labelled as such.
